## Purpose and boundaries

Complete the physical **mine → vertical skip → freight railway** link: an upright ore skip lifts or lowers large single-commodity loads using the **existing M05/M06 winch and cable system**, and transfers freight to/from the existing **M06 Loading Track** through visibly appropriate doors, ramp and chute. Build no compulsory second station, conveyor, generic logistics automation, generator, or new power network. The physical railway, shaft, support timbering, elevated rail placement and route geometry should do the work.

## 1. Skip physical form, collision and visual clearance

- **Upright skip body:** exactly **one block wide × one block long × three blocks tall (1×1×3)** in its primary *body collision envelope*. It is **three blocks high, not three blocks long**. The upper **two body blocks** are the ore receptacle; a **sliding discharge gate at their floor** meters ore into the chute. The **first/bottom body block** is an **open-air angled side-discharge chute**, visibly open, integral to the carrier; it is *not* a separate chamber or mandatory station block.
- **Fourth-block bridle:** a **triangular two-leg cable bridle** attaches on both sides of the upper ore intake, converging at **one apex** above the skip. It occupies the **fourth vertical block counting from the bottom**, immediately above the three-block body, and **HAS collision**. Treat it as a moving, solid part of the skip; do not render it as a non-colliding ghost that passes through beams, ceilings, rails or other world geometry. The bridle is not an extra fourth storage/body block.
- **Sideways-minecart visual:** the skip's *visible silhouette* resembles a **rectangular minecart turned sideways** and therefore extends laterally beyond its logical 1×1 body collision footprint. **Require a clear block of room on EACH lateral side** of its travel path, in the axis along which the artwork extends, to avoid clipping with surrounding terrain. Keep body collision 1×1×3; explicitly check these flanking visual-clearance cells **without falsely making the whole skip a three-block-wide physical hitbox**. Select/document the facing/visual lateral axis in the implementation. Validate the extra fourth-block bridle and door-deployment clearance as well; never let animations clip through solid blocks.
- The skip moves **only vertically**, staying upright under its hoist wheel; **no horizontal swinging, tipping, pendulum physics, guide rail or counterweight** required. Collisions with shaft ends, walls, the bridle, the visually required side clearance and unloaded geometry stop motion safely, rather than teleporting the skip or permitting phasing.
- Use editable **Blockbench `.bbmodel` sources**, paintable/replaceable UV texture templates and normal Minecraft art discipline; do not overwrite owner-authored assets. Prefer visibly distinct gate, top opening, bridle and lower chute, with readable alignment relative to the loading track. Precise collider subdivisions, interpolation, model transforms and pivot/facing are delegated to Claude, subject to the above constraints.

## 2. Cargo, inventory screen and crafting

- **One commodity at a time, total capacity 512 items.** The skip has a **single actual cargo slot** shown in an inventory screen; **THAT ONE SLOT** supports an exceptional stack count up to **512**. The item type and honest 0–512 count are shown; do not substitute eight normal 64-stack slots, nine invisible slots, a 512-slot container, mixed cargo, or merely a text label unrelated to the actual inventory state.
- **Keep this overstacking exception local to skip storage.** Normal item stacks in the player's inventory, cursors, hotbar, ordinary containers, dropped items and the vanilla minecart's existing **256-item single-commodity freight inventory** retain their existing rules. UI click, shift-click, drag, cursor handling, deposit/withdraw, full/empty operations, slot filtering, death/dismantle/drop and multiplayer synchronization must all conserve counts and obey ordinary destination slot capacities. A 512-item skip stack must **not escape intact into a normal 64-item inventory slot** or disappear when split. Use appropriate bounded transfer/batching/safe refusal; Claude owns the implementation method.
- When empty, accept any **eligible bulk ore/commodity** already supported by Simple Block's freight eligibility; when occupied, accept **only the SAME commodity**. Do not invent a new mixed-ore policy or broaden the game's eligible material categories. A full skip does not accept more; wrong-type input is rejected without replacing its stored type. Neither hand interaction nor powered transfer may duplicate or void freight. Validate serialization and recovery after save/load and reconnect.
- **Owner-settled shaped recipe (3×3; exact occupied cells and empty cells matter):**

  ```text
  [empty]        [cable]         [empty]
  [empty]        [iron trapdoor] [empty]
  [iron ingot]   [minecart]      [iron ingot]
  ```

  Use the existing Simple Block **Cable item** and ordinary vanilla **iron trapdoor, minecart, and two iron ingots**; earlier design shorthand `iron` means iron ingot (not an iron block). No new intermediates, extra construction machine or invented material charge. **Conventional starting yield: one skip per craft**, subject to owner balancing after playtest. Place/craft the skip without replacing the original vanilla minecart's identity. Verify shaped placement and negative recipes with misplaced/missing ingredients.
- Provide an intuitive way to **open the skip inventory** without interfering with using the loose cable hook to attach the bridle. Right-click/interaction gestures and the precise UI layout are delegated to Claude. Ensure a loaded skip cannot be dismantled or broken in a way that silently loses freight; return or safely spill/batch the contents under the existing item-stack rules.

## 3. One main cable and vertical hoisting

- Reuse the **existing copper anchors, wooden supports, routed cable, loose hook, overhead guide wheel and M05/M06 installed winch drum**. Using the **loose cable hook on the skip** creates/attaches the triangular bridle; two visible bridle legs meet at **one apex**, and from that apex **ONE main hoisting cable** runs toward the wheel and drum. Do not route two full-length independent winch cables.
- Apply M06's settled rule: **one installed reusable Cable Coil provides unlimited cumulative route length**; **no extra coil or cable items** are charged for a taller shaft, the two short bridle legs or returning/repositioning cable. Proper dismantling/attachment must not create new coils or cable items proportional to distance. Preserve real copper/wood supports, the existing **maximum 12-block unsupported span** where applicable to supported horizontal cable runs, and valid physical route/collision requirements; do not reinterpret the 12-block horizontal anchor rule as a demand to put fixed supports every 12 vertical blocks along the hanging moving hoist.
- The skip travels **straight up/down under its hoist wheel**, using the *normal available speed/force* of the connected existing winch, subject to its load and the shaft's real clearance. **Manual M05 winch:** hold-left FEED/away, hold-right PULL/toward; letting go holds. **Furnace-upgraded M06 winch:** lever physically back PULL/toward drum, forward FEED/away, neutral stops; right-click cycles **NEUTRAL → PULL → FEED → NEUTRAL**, including safe direct reversal. M07 integrates vertical skip movement with these existing controls; it adds **no special skip engine, GUI movement button, new power type or recurring transport program**.
- At shaft top/bottom, obstruction, incompatible docking geometry or other genuine endpoint/transfer stops, the skip **halts and holds securely**. An M06 powered lever flips visibly to **NEUTRAL** on a physical stop. Powered winch coal burns **only during actual cable-driven motion**, preserving the exact remainder of a partly consumed fuel unit on stops, at stations and across save/load; the operator must explicitly select a direction again to resume. The M05 **Cobblestone Engine** has a different owner-settled stall-burn rule; do not apply locomotive burning behavior to this winch.
- **Chunk safety:** respect the eventual M06 solution for faraway non-ticking cable/anchors. Do not simulate or teleport unloaded skips, force-load an entire shaft, discard bridle/route state because a chunk temporarily unloads, or move into unknown collision geometry. If required active geometry is unavailable, halt safely, preserve cargo/cable/fuel and report runtime limitations rather than pretending background travel occurs.

## 4. Loading Track transfers — elevation dictates the direction

The existing **M06 Loading Track** remains the only freight transfer station. It already locks an ordinary non-cushioned cart and stops/holds its entire coupled consist, with release by **right-clicking the track**. M07 adds the skip-aware docking, opening animations and *actual cargo movement*; a cushioned passenger cart is **not** a freight-transfer target.

**Owner-settled, non-negotiable rule: use the section of the skip the waiting freight cart is ALIGNED BESIDE to determine direction.**

| Height of docked freight cart relative to skip | Transfer mode | Physical animation/interface |
|---|---|---|
| **FOURTH-block collidable bridle** (above the 1×1×3 body) | **Cart → skip**: freight cart is the **source**, dumping into the skip | Skip top intake opens and the cart's M06 folding front panel **unfolds downward as a ramp** into the intake when clearance permits |
| **FIRST/BOTTOM skip body block** (lower angled chute) | **Skip → cart**: skip is the **source**, cart is the recipient | Skip's sliding upper-container floor gate releases material onto the integral lower chute and into the waiting cart; the cart front **stays CLOSED** |

- **Do NOT** determine transfer direction based on an empty space in front of the cart, whether the skip is currently loaded/empty, or whichever inventory has cargo. The old provisional assumption “empty skip means loading; loaded skip means unloading” is **superseded**. A skip with ore at bridle-height docking is still the **destination**, and an empty skip at bottom-chute docking is still the **source**; if materials/capacity are not compatible, **do not transfer**. A clear frontage block is relevant to physically safe ramp *deployment*, **not** to selecting the direction.
- Do not transfer for other skip heights or merely because a track/cart happens to be close. A cart must be truly stopped/held on a correctly faced M06 loading track, with the appropriate skip section horizontally adjacent and locally aligned; maintain meaningful collision and visibility/clearance, including the **fourth-block bridle collider and lateral visual envelope**. Do not clip a ramp through blocks, run ore through a wall or open a cargo door on a random air gap.
- A docked transfer is an **actual stop**, not a drive-through. At valid station height, stop the skip, stop the powered winch/flip its lever to NEUTRAL, and keep existing locomotive train locks consistent; transferring is not permission to let the engine push a parked cart. The player can later **right-click the M06 Loading Track to release the cart** and **deliberately command the winch** to continue; neither station nor skip automatically drives a return trip.
- Transfer only **eligible matching commodity** and **as much as the receiver has room for** (cart capacity 256; skip capacity 512). Partial transfers are legitimate; incompatible type, insufficient capacity, missing source, premature departure, obstruction, chunk unload or broken station **never destroy or duplicate items**. A blocked/unavailable destination safely leaves remaining cargo in the source. Doors/gates close or retract appropriately before either assembly departs.
- **Claude owns** local detection radius, alignment/snap tolerance, physically safe small positioning adjustments, animation staging, discrete transfer rate and timing, and how the station is latched through a transfer. Prefer a bounded server-authoritative state machine, predictable positioning and player-readable feedback over long-distance snapping or elaborate infrastructure. Tests must prove both intended height states, *negative controls at the wrong height*, collision rejection, incompatible cargo, partial and full destinations, mid-transfer unload/save/reopen and interrupted operations.
