# M14 — The Old Coot: a territorial abandoned-mineshaft miner

**Owner design settled 2026-10-20. PLANNED - READY; this milestone is NOT implementation authorization.** The Old Coot is an uncommon, semi-hostile, persistent underground NPC—not a new ore, tool tier, progression gate, villager profession, or ordinary hostile-mob replacement. He has lived in an abandoned mineshaft long enough to regard its ores and cave spiders as *his*. The exact numeric tuning below can be refined with real playtesting, but the behavior and visual identity are owner-directed.

## Appearance, identity and atmosphere

- **Name:** Old Coot (working/favored name; “Hermit Miner” was an earlier idea, not a second mob).
- **Silhouette and height:** Unmistakably **villager-like**, but **short, squat and broad like a dwarf**, standing **about 1¼ blocks tall (1.25 blocks / 20 Minecraft model pixels)** rather than a normal-height villager. Model and collision/interaction hitbox should reflect this approximately 1.25-block height. **His nose MUST specifically be a recognizable Minecraft VILLAGER NOSE**, modeled as the characteristic protruding block-shaped villager nose in **actual 3D geometry**. A merely prominent human/dwarf nose, generic face, painted nose on a flat surface or texture-only illusion **does not meet this requirement**. Keep the nose clearly visible below his hat and in front of the beard, with Minecraft-style blocky proportions.
- **Distinctive art:** An **enormous SCRAGGLY white beard** with uneven clumps, irregular ends and an unkempt outline, a battered old **miner's hat**, worn work clothing and an **iron pickaxe**. The beard must look rough and poorly maintained **in its model silhouette**, not like a tidy triangular wizard beard or neatly groomed dwarf beard with a scruffy texture painted over smooth geometry. The **villager nose**, scraggly beard, squat proportions and hat must all read clearly together from normal gameplay distance.
- **Behavioral atmosphere:** Mostly quiet and absorbed in his work; intermittent **audible, realistic pickaxe impacts on stone/deepslate**, footsteps and occasional grumbling establish his presence **before he is seen**, including when he is tunneling out of sight beyond a corner or through nearby rock. A player hearing mining in an apparently abandoned corridor should plausibly be startled. Sounds follow **actual mining swings/impacts and game-world distance**, not a constant ambient loop or an impossible worldwide warning. A clear angry response may accompany provocation. No forced trading, dialogue, quests or village affiliation are required.
- **Visible pickaxe-arm animation:** The arm **holding his iron pickaxe must physically swing** during **both actual stone/deepslate mining and melee attacks against a player**. A static-held pickaxe, body jolt or invisible-damage attack does not satisfy this. Synchronize mining impact and combat damage cues with the arm swing.
- **Art ownership:** Claude supplies working entity geometry, animation, UVs, placeholder assets, and render/client tests only after implementation authorization; Joe owns final paint and approval. See [Joe's painting queue](Joe's%20To-Do/BLOCKBENCH_PAINTING.md). Do not overwrite owner art.

## Natural spawn and population rules

1. **Abandoned mineshafts only.** Natural spawn points must lie **inside a generated abandoned-mineshaft structure**, not in arbitrary caves, player-built tunnels, ordinary villages, or nearby open rock merely because a mineshaft is close.
2. **Any light level.** He can naturally spawn **inside an eligible abandoned mineshaft regardless of lighting**: darkness, partial light and brightly lit positions are all valid. **Do not require a minimum light level or apply a darkness-only restriction**; existing torches are not a prerequisite. All other placement, structure, capacity and rarity rules remain in force.
3. **Maximum one living Old Coot per individual mineshaft**, **not one per entire world**. Distinct mineshafts may each have one. Keep an authoritative, persistent identity/occupancy record per generated mineshaft so chunk unload/reload, player movement, multiple players and server restarts cannot create duplicates. Do not rely only on nearby loaded-entity counts.
4. **Staple, not guaranteed — approximately two-thirds of mineshafts.** Target **roughly 2 in 3 explored abandoned mineshafts** as potential homes with a resident Old Coot: encountered often enough to be associated with mineshafts but **not every mineshaft**. Assign/persist each individual generated mineshaft's **resident eligibility once**, rather than giving every currently empty mineshaft endlessly repeating initial spawn rolls that converge to 100% occupancy over time. A selected home can spawn its resident when a valid loaded location is available; a nonselected home remains naturally vacant. Tune the target probability and initial opportunity timing based on actual playtesting without overriding this distribution goal. **After a resident dies**, wait at least **7 Minecraft days (168,000 game ticks; approximately 2h 20m of actively advancing world time)** before another can spawn in that home, then make **low-probability periodic respawn attempts** (not a guaranteed instant replacement at expiry). Being unloaded is **not** death and must not start/reset cooldown. Never duplicate a still-living but unloaded resident.
5. **Persistent residents.** Once spawned, he survives normal entity despawn conditions and retains his home-mineshaft association across save/reload. No global ticking of unloaded mineshafts, forced chunk loading, world-spanning structure rescans every tick, or offscreen autonomous mining. Maintain a bounded, reliable way to reconcile genuine death/removal versus temporary unloading.
6. Natural spawning must respect server authority, dimension/structure data and ordinary safety checks (valid space, ground, collisions). **The natural-spawn quota applies to naturally selected residents, not explicitly spawned Coots**; creative egg or command/test summoning follows the separately defined manual-spawn rules below. No creative spawn or summon may alter the natural mineshaft ledger.

## Everyday behavior: the mine is his home

- **Neutral until provoked:** He can walk past a player without attacking; he does not object to walking, looting minecart/chests or breaking ordinary stone.
- **Mindless, genuine tunneling:** When peaceful, he wanders at approximately **zombie walking speed** through his home area, intermittently chooses **nearby reachable exposed ordinary stone or deepslate**, and **actually mines** it with visibly timed iron-pickaxe arm swings and matching audible impacts. His aim is not efficient mining, ore targeting or a perfectly planned tunnel system: he haphazardly extends **small side passages, recesses and dead ends** from around his existing mineshaft. Sometimes he will be **out of sight down a corridor or beyond a wall while clearly audible working**, making an apparently abandoned mine unnerving. Genuine blocks disappear from the world; no cosmetic-only mining or looping sound against intact rock. **Mine-destroyed stone/deepslate produces NO dropped cobblestone, cobbled deepslate or XP**: prevent unbounded item entities and automated free-stone farming.
- **Simple strict material/radius rule, not a player-built carve-out:** Only **ordinary stone** and **ordinary deepslate** are mineable, **within a bounded home working territory** around a persistent mineshaft anchor (initial tuning target: **24–32 block radius**, not a world-wide mineshaft scan). Nothing else may be mined: **no ores**, cobblestone, cobbled deepslate, other stone variants, rail blocks, minecart tracks, wood/supports, chests, spawners, torches, machines or other materials. **Do not add special player-placed-stone tracking or a categorical 'player-made structures cannot be touched' exemption.** If the player builds using actual ordinary stone/deepslate **inside his working radius**, those blocks are eligible under the same material rule; this is intentional. His fixed territory, slow intermittent mining and strict block whitelist are the protection against widespread griefing. Avoid obviously unsafe fluid breakthroughs, unsupported drops/shafts or a direct bypass of indestructible/protected blocks without installing a speculative universal structure classifier. Mine only while the entity and relevant chunks normally tick; do not excavate elsewhere or offscreen in unloaded areas.
- **Occasional real torches:** Rarely place an **actual torch** at a safe valid position near his little tunnels, without overwriting blocks or destroying mineshaft infrastructure. Sparse light pools and the sound of mining are environmental clues. Bound both placement frequency **and cumulative torch generation per resident/home**, so a player cannot farm unlimited free torches by collecting placed ones. Exact modest cap and interval are tuneable; avoid rapid decorating, offscreen chunk work and item/entity accumulation.
- **Monster neutrality, both directions:** **All ordinary hostile mobs ignore the Old Coot, and he ignores them**; cave spiders are included. They neither proactively target nor attack each other. This is not a global pacification of hostile mobs toward the player.

## What makes him angry

- **Ore theft — audible through walls at ~15 blocks:** When a player actually mines/breaks **coal, iron, diamond, lapis lazuli, redstone, gold or emerald ore**, including corresponding deepslate ore variants, within **about 15 blocks** (3D local hearing distance; owner-preferred initial tuning), he targets that player **even without line of sight**: he hears you stealing *his* ore through nearby rock. Do not require visual contact, detect faraway mining, or respond to merely holding ore. Stone and deepslate excavation by players does not provoke him.
- **Defending cave spiders:** If the Old Coot has **direct line of sight to a player who attacks a cave spider**, he attacks that player. The trigger is **seeing the player's attack on the cave spider**, not merely a cave spider taking unexplained damage, the player defending against another monster, or the spider existing nearby. Line-of-sight and short-range tests matter.
- **Self-defense:** A player directly attacking the Old Coot also provokes him, even if no ore or cave spider was involved.
- **Hostile response, speed and toughness:** Start at **villager-like health: 20 HP (10 hearts)**, with **up to ~24 HP** only if playtesting shows he needs to be slightly tougher; he is **not** a boss or armored tank. His melee hit deals about the **same damage as a player swinging an iron pickaxe**, respecting sensible attack timing/cooldowns (not arbitrary huge mob damage). He **stops mining**, moves at approximately **spider movement speed while attacking**, and **visibly swings his pickaxe-holding arm** to deal actual melee damage linked to the animation. Once provoked, pursuit is **Enderman-like in persistence**: stubbornly chase and try to reacquire the player instead of giving up instantly if they round a corner, until one dies or the player genuinely escapes out of tracking/chase range. **No Enderman teleportation or special attacks.** Working territory remains bounded; pursuit may reach farther than the digging radius but must eventually break if the player escapes or exceeds a sensible home-linked leash.
- **Calm again after a genuine escape:** A briefly broken line of sight does not reset his anger. If the player outruns his extended chase/home leash and he truly loses track of them, he **ends pursuit and returns home to neutral wandering and mining**. He must not permanently blacklist the player, demand a peace offering, flee/surrender, teleport, or remain hostile forever solely because of a past encounter. Set reasonable chase and loss-of-target hysteresis during tuning; no flickering between calm and angry every few ticks.
- Other hostile mobs remain neutral with him during the chase. Do not let player-versus-Coot combat or cave-spider protection create unintended persistent monster-to-Coot aggro.

## Death drops and economy

- **1–3 emeralds** on death (owner-accepted starting drop proposal); maintain the intended modest handful, never a large renewable emerald fountain.
- **Exactly one iron pickaxe**, with randomized **5–50% durability REMAINING** (i.e., 50–95% used), not 5–50% damage taken and not an undamaged pickaxe. Clamp rounding to a valid non-broken item, and verify tooltip/durability-bar display.
- Death starts the home mineshaft's **7-day minimum respawn cooldown**. Kill-and-reload, chunk boundaries and multiple attackers must not duplicate the reward or the living resident.
