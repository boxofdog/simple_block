# Project Vision — Minecraft as a Hostile Frontier

## Core fantasy

The player begins in a dangerous wilderness and gradually makes land safe, productive, and connected. Walls, illuminated settlements, developed mines, and transport infrastructure are valuable because they change what the player can safely and efficiently do in the world.

**Core loop:** Explore → Secure → Extract → Transport → Develop → Expand.

The mod is not intended to be a generic hard-mode survival pack. It removes friction that is tedious while restoring consequences to things that vanilla conveniences bypass.

## Pillars

1. **Territory matters.** An enclosed, lit, defended area provides reliable safety; wilderness, especially after dark, remains threatening.
2. **The physical world matters.** Travel distance, topography, ore geography, mine access, and bulk freight cannot simply be bypassed by sleep, wings, or portable containers.
3. **Infrastructure matters.** Rails, cargo carts, overhead haulage, skips, and useful manual machinery transform work from inefficient to satisfying.
4. **Progression matters.** Resource and equipment tiers should take meaningful effort; finding major ore deposits should be a memorable discovery.
5. **Austerity is selective.** Hunger should be more forgiving, menus more usable, and junk content reduced. Resist meters, micromanagement, and arbitrary punishments.
6. **Mechanization, not autonomous industry.** Player-operated physical devices may transport, transfer, or process material; large-scale unattended resource production should be severely limited.
7. **Minecraft remains Minecraft.** Prefer intuitive blocks, physical construction, and emergent possibilities to quests, RPG skill trees, and abstract progression gates.

## Desired feeling

Looking from a fortified, illuminated settlement toward dangerous surrounding country should communicate a meaningful change: this place is livable because the player built it. A long-distance railroad or properly equipped mine should feel rewarding to use, not merely decorative.

## Non-goals

- Starvation/thirst/temperature simulation or elaborate resource-management chores.
- Cave-in punishment for failing to install timber supports.
- Redstone-style programmable automation recreated with differently textured wires.
- Permanent sieges and mob harassment as an end in themselves.
- Highly realistic but tedious physics at the expense of understandable, fun machines.

## Development strategy

Build small, playable increments. Future systems in this vision are context, not implementation authority. Milestone 01 intentionally changes only four vanilla survival features.
